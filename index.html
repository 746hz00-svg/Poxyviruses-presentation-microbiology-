Introduction to Poxviruses

  • Poxviruses are the largest and most complex of all medically significant viruses.
  • They are unique among DNA viruses because they replicate entirely in the host cell cytoplasm.
  • Historical Significance: They caused Smallpox (Variola), the only infectious disease of humans to be completely eradicated by global vaccination.
  • Current Importance: Resurgence of Zoonotic infections (e.g., Monkeypox) and widespread presence of Molluscum contagiosum.
Microbiology Viva Concept
Poxviruses do not need to enter the host nucleus because they carry their own DNA-dependent RNA polymerase, allowing transcription in the cytoplasm.

Classification of Poxviruses

Family: Poxviridae

Divided into two subfamilies: Chordopoxvirinae (vertebrates) and Entomopoxvirinae (insects).

Morphology of Poxviruses

  • Size: 200–300 nm × 250–350 nm (Large enough to be seen under a light microscope).
  • Shape: Brick-shaped or oval (Parapoxviruses are ovoid).
  • Symmetry: Complex symmetry (Neither strictly icosahedral nor helical).
  • Core: Dumbbell-shaped core containing the viral DNA.
  • Lateral Bodies: Two proteinaceous structures situated in the concavities of the core.
  • Envelope: Complex outer membrane showing surface ridges or "tubules".
Transmission Electron Micrograph (TEM) of a Poxvirus virion. Note the brick shape and complex surface ridges.

Genome & Replication Overview

  • Genome: Linear, double-stranded DNA (dsDNA). Ends are covalently closed (hairpin loops).
  • Site of Replication: Host cell cytoplasm.

Steps of Replication:

  1. Entry: Fusion with plasma membrane.
  2. Uncoating: Occurs in two stages. First stage removes outer membrane, second stage releases DNA (requires viral uncoating protein).
  3. Synthesis: Viral replication occurs in isolated localized cytoplasmic structures known as "viral factories" or Guarnieri bodies.
  4. Assembly & Release: Assembled in cytoplasm, wrapped in Golgi membrane, released by cell lysis or exocytosis.

Important Clinical Cases & Diseases

  • Smallpox (Variola): Historically fatal. Rash evolved sequentially: Macule → Papule → Vesicle → Pustule → Crust. Centrifugal distribution.
  • Monkeypox (Mpox): Zoonotic. Clinically resembles smallpox but features prominent Lymphadenopathy (swollen lymph nodes) before the rash.
  • Molluscum Contagiosum: Benign epidermal tumors. Pearly, flesh-colored, dome-shaped papules with central umbilication.
Classic vesiculopustular lesions seen in Monkeypox virus infection.

Laboratory Diagnosis

  • Microscopy (Histopathology): Staining of tissue samples to observe specific viral inclusion bodies.
    • Guarnieri bodies (Smallpox/Vaccinia): Intracytoplasmic, eosinophilic.
    • Henderson-Paterson bodies (Molluscum): Large hyaline masses in epidermal cells.
  • Electron Microscopy (EM): Rapid identification of poxvirus particles from vesicular fluid.
  • Molecular Methods: PCR is the gold standard for specific diagnosis (e.g., differentiating Mpox from Varicella).
  • Viral Isolation: Growth on Chorioallantoic Membrane (CAM) of chick embryos produces specific "pocks".
H&E Stain showing large, distinct Henderson-Paterson inclusion bodies (Molluscum bodies).

Prevention and Control

  • Smallpox Eradication: Achieved through a global vaccination campaign using the live, attenuated Vaccinia virus. Declared eradicated in 1980.
  • Mpox Vaccination: JYNNEOS vaccine (Modified Vaccinia Ankara - MVA) is a non-replicating viral vector vaccine currently used for Monkeypox prevention.
  • Cross-Immunity: Infection or vaccination with one Orthopoxvirus (like Vaccinia) provides substantial protection against others (like Variola or Monkeypox).
  • Molluscum Management: Often self-limiting. Treated via cryotherapy, curettage, or topical agents (Cantharidin) in severe/cosmetic cases.

Exam & Viva Q&A Focus

Q1: How does Poxvirus morphology differ from other viruses?
Answer: It is the largest virus, possesses "complex" symmetry (neither strictly icosahedral nor helical), has a brick/oval shape, and contains a dumbbell-shaped core with lateral bodies.
Q2: Why do DNA poxviruses replicate in the cytoplasm?
Answer: Unlike other DNA viruses that require host nuclear enzymes, poxviruses carry their own DNA-dependent RNA polymerase and enzymes needed for DNA replication.
Q3: Name the inclusion bodies associated with Poxviruses.
Answer: Guarnieri bodies (Vaccinia/Variola) and Henderson-Paterson bodies (Molluscum contagiosum). Both are intracytoplasmic.
Q4: Clinical difference between Smallpox and Chickenpox?
Answer: Smallpox rash is centrifugal (dense on limbs/face), lesions are deep-seated, and all lesions in one area are at the same stage of development. Chickenpox (Herpesvirus) is centripetal and lesions show pleomorphism (different stages at once).
